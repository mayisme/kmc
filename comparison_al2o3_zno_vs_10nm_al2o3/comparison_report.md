# KMC comparison: Al2O3/ZnO bilayer vs 10 nm Al2O3

| Metric | 4.5 nm Al2O3 / 6 nm ZnO | 10 nm Al2O3 | Bilayer advantage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ptrans | 0.1833 | 1 | 5.45554x lower |
| FPT mean / s | 4258.36 | 0.655689 | 6494.48x higher |
| FPT median / s | 4173.4 | 0.514337 | 8114.13x higher |
| Lpath mean / mm | 1.20429 | 0.00331414 | 363.379x higher |
| Lpath median / mm | 1.18127 | 0.0015915 | 742.239x higher |
| tau mean | 114694 | 331.414 | 346.075x higher |
| tau median | 112502 | 159.15 | 706.895x higher |
| D_eff / m2 s-1 | 1.29451e-20 | 7.62557e-17 | 5890.68x lower |
| WVTR ideal vapor / g m-2 day-1 | 4.42637e-06 | 0.0273781 | 6185.22x lower |
| WVTR sorbed C1 / g m-2 day-1 | 0.00072114 | 4.46041 | 6185.22x lower |

Interpretation: under the same pinhole geometry scale, the single Al2O3 layer provides a direct pinhole pathway, whereas the bilayer forces molecules leaving Al2O3 pinholes to search a ZnO grain-boundary network. This lowers Ptrans, lengthens FPT and Lpath, increases tortuosity, and lowers D_eff/WVTR.
